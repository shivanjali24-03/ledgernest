# LedgerNest feature list

LedgerNest is accounting software for any business: a shop, a clinic, a school, an agency, a factory or a non-profit. It is built in phases. Each feature becomes a GitHub issue, and each issue ships through a pull request.

Status: ✅ done · 🚧 in progress · 📋 planned

### Built-in roles

| Role | Level | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Super Admin** | Platform | Everything across all organisations: create/suspend organisations, manage platform users, view platform audit log |
| **Owner** | Organisation | Everything in their organisation, including billing settings and deleting the organisation |
| **Admin** | Organisation | Manage users, roles and settings; full accounting access |
| **Accountant** | Organisation | Full accounting: journals, period close, reports, tax |
| **Bookkeeper** | Organisation | Day-to-day entry: invoices, bills, payments, bank; cannot post to closed periods or change settings |
| **Auditor** | Organisation | Read-only access to all books, reports and the audit log |
| **Viewer** | Organisation | Read-only dashboards and reports |

## Phase 3: Sales and purchases

| Feature | What it means | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Customers and vendors | Contacts with addresses, tax IDs (GSTIN checked, giving the state of supply), currency, payment terms, and what each owes or is owed | ✅ #64 |
| Addresses from the PIN code | Indian addresses for contacts and the organisation start from the PIN code: India Post's directory (through the API, which keeps each answer for 30 days) fills the state from the GST list, and the city or town and the area are picked from its taluks and post offices instead of typed. Typing is offered only when the PIN isn't known or the directory can't be reached. Addresses outside India stay free text | ✅ |
| Products and services | Items with prices, tax defaults, HSN/SAC codes and income/expense accounts | ✅ #65 |
| Packed goods details | Goods take their unit from GST's list of units of quantity (UQC: NOS, KGS, PAC…), so GSTR-1, e-invoices and e-way bills carry it as is; the MRP printed on the pack (a sale price above it is refused); a barcode, unique in the organisation and checked for EAN/UPC check digits, that a scanner can type into the item search; a brand and a category, suggested from those in use and filterable. In the items CSV and its import | ✅ |
| Batches and expiry dates | Goods whose stock is tracked can be kept in batches (turned on before any stock moves): a bill line records the batch number with its manufacturing and expiry dates (the same batch with other dates is refused), sales take the earliest-expiring batch first or the one chosen on the line, and expired stock is never sold. Credit notes return goods to the batches they left, voids put them back batch by batch, and stock counts and write-offs name the batch. Cost stays at the item's weighted average. The invoice, its PDF and the bill show each batch with its dates | ✅ |
| Expiring stock | The inventory page lists batches with stock that have expired or expire within 30, 60, 90 or 180 days, with days left, quantity and value at average cost, expired ones first and marked; the dashboard warns when any batch has expired or expires within 30 days (GET /inventory/expiring) | ✅ |
| Serial numbers | Goods whose stock is tracked can record a serial number for each unit (IMEI, machine number; not with batches): bills list the units bought, invoices the units sold (printed on the invoice and its PDF), returns tick the units coming back from those sold, and stock counts and write-offs name them. A unit can't come in twice or go out unless it's in stock. Any serial can be traced from the inventory page: the item, where the unit is now, who it was bought from and sold to (GET /inventory/serials/:serial) | ✅ |
| Opening stock | One list of every tracked item to enter what was on hand when the books start and what one cost (with batch and expiry, or serial numbers, where the item needs them), saved together against Opening balance equity, all or nothing (POST /inventory/opening-stock) | ✅ |
| Second units | Goods can also be sold or bought in a second unit (boxes of 12 pieces): an invoice or bill line picks the unit and its price follows, stock stays in the main unit, returns use the line's unit, and the invoice, e-invoice and GSTR-1 HSN summary carry the right unit and quantity | ✅ |
| Invoices | Printable (PDF) GST invoices with your terms and bank details at the foot, emailed to the customer as a PDF, numbering, due dates, place of supply; receipts against one or many invoices, with advances and realised exchange differences | ✅ #66, #69 |
| Number series | Invoices, credit notes and debit notes numbered from series set in Settings: a prefix and suffix around the running number (with the financial year as {FY}, {FY2}, {YYYY} or {YY}), its digits, a start number, and whether it starts again each financial year. Several series per type (e.g. exports, a branch), one the default, chosen on the invoice. Series that would give numbers over GST's 16 characters, or with other characters than letters, digits, - and /, are refused. A number is written onto the document when issued, so changing a series never renumbers anything; used series are archived, not deleted. GSTR-1 Table 13 (documents issued) lists each series' first and last number, total and cancelled, in the screen and the portal JSON | ✅ |
| Pay by UPI | Set the organisation's UPI ID once: every rupee invoice with something still owed then carries a UPI QR code for the balance (on screen, in the PDF, in the emailed invoice and in reminders) that Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM or any bank app scans with the amount and invoice number filled in. No gateway, no fees, money straight to the bank | ✅ |
| Invoice links and WhatsApp | Share an issued invoice as a private link the customer opens without an account: what's owed and when, a Pay by UPI button and QR code, the PDF, the full invoice, in English, Hindi or Marathi. Sent with one tap on WhatsApp (to the customer's mobile when it's on file) with the message written; the invoice shows when the customer opened it; links can be turned off, stop when the invoice is voided, and are never copied into restored backups | ✅ |
| Quote links | Share a quote as a link (a draft becomes sent) on WhatsApp or copied: the customer opens it without an account, sees the lines and totals, downloads the PDF and accepts or declines with their name and a note while it's open; the quote moves, records who decided, and quote webhooks fire. Opens are counted, expired quotes can't be answered, links can be turned off | ✅ |
| Interest on late payment | Interest terms set for the organisation (a yearly rate, simple or compounded monthly, and days of grace), changed or turned off per customer, or set on one invoice; an invoice keeps the terms it was issued with and prints them. Interest is worked out day by day on a 365-day year from the due date: part-payments and credit notes reduce what bears interest from their day, payments go to what's owed first, and nothing is charged if paid within the grace. ₹1,00,000 due 1 June at 18% with ₹40,000 paid 1 July: ₹1,479.45, then ₹29.59 a day on the ₹60,000 left. The invoice page shows what's accrued and what it grows by each day | ✅ |
| Interest shown where it's owed | Interest accrued under each invoice's terms appears in the aging report (per invoice and customer), on Collections, in statements of account (to the end date, beside the balance, not in it, and on the PDF), on the customer's own link (per invoice and in total, beside the MSMED figure) and in payment reminder emails (so far, and what it grows by each day) | ✅ |
| Interest invoices and waivers | The interest left to charge on an invoice (accrued, less what's been invoiced and waived) is raised as a draft invoice to the customer, with GST at the rates of the supply it's on split by taxable value (CGST Act s.15(2)(d)) and booked to Interest on late payment; it carries no interest of its own. Interest can be waived with a reason, kept on record. Collections raises drafts for everything due at once, and an organisation can have them drafted on the 1st of each month, to the end of the month just gone (INTEREST_RUN_MINUTES) | ✅ |
| Interest report | Reports → Interest: every invoice with interest on late payment on a day, with the customer, days late, rate, interest accrued, invoiced, waived and left to charge, the totals and what it grows by each day; CSV (GET /reports/interest) | ✅ |
| Instalments | An invoice can be paid in instalments: split into equal monthly ones from a first day (odd paise on the last) and any day or amount changed; they must add up to the total and not fall due before the invoice, and the last is the due date. Payments clear the oldest first; the invoice is overdue only once an instalment past its day isn't covered; interest on late payment runs on each instalment from its own day. The invoice, its PDF and the customer's link show the plan and the next instalment | ✅ |
| Order and dispatch details | An invoice can carry the buyer's order number and date, delivery note, dispatch document or LR number, transporter, destination, vehicle number (checked and tidied) and terms of delivery; they print on the invoice and its PDF, and the e-way bill takes the vehicle and transporter from them | ✅ |
| Ship-to address | An invoice can ship to another address (prefilled from the customer's shipping address, picked from the PIN code); its state becomes the place of supply, as goods are supplied where they're delivered, and can still be changed for a third party (bill to, ship to). It prints on the invoice and PDF, e-invoices carry it as ShipDtls, and e-way bills go Bill To - Ship To with the goods' destination | ✅ |
| Round off and copies | Rupee invoice totals can be rounded to the nearest rupee (a Settings choice): the difference shows as Round off on the invoice, its PDF and e-invoice (RndOffAmt), and is booked to its own Round off account. Goods invoices download as the Original for recipient, Duplicate for transporter or Triplicate for supplier copy (CGST rule 48) | ✅ |
| Prices with GST, invoice discounts, HSN summary | An invoice's prices can include GST, taken out of each line; a discount on the whole invoice (a percentage or an amount) is spread over the lines by value before tax, lowering the taxable value as GST allows; the invoice, its PDF and e-invoice show them (unit prices go to the portal without tax), with an HSN/SAC summary of taxable value and tax by code and rate | ✅ |
| Compensation cess | Cess on top of GST for the goods that still carry it after GST 2.0 (tobacco and pan masala at 28%): the Taxes page adds a cess rate to a GST rate, making cess input and output accounts and a group within the state (CGST + SGST + cess) and one between states (IGST + cess); invoices, bills, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, e-invoices and e-way bills carry the cess | ✅ |
| Exports and SEZ supplies | Each invoice has a type of supply: regular, export or SEZ with IGST, export or SEZ under a letter of undertaking (no IGST), or deemed export. Exports have no Indian place of supply and carry the shipping bill, port and country; exports and SEZ supplies are always IGST; zero-rated ones need a LUT (Settings: ARN and validity) covering the invoice date. The invoice prints the declaration the law asks for, GSTR-1 files them under exports (WPAY/WOPAY) or B2B as SEWP/SEWOP/DE, and e-invoices use the matching supply type with export details | ✅ |
| Reverse charge on sales | For services the buyer pays GST on (goods transport by a GTA, legal services to a business and the like), an invoice can be marked reverse charge: no GST is charged or posted, but the rate is kept, so the invoice says "tax payable on reverse charge: Yes" with the GST the buyer pays, GSTR-1 files it as B2B with reverse charge and that tax, GSTR-3B leaves it out of output tax, and the e-invoice is marked RegRev Y. Only for registered buyers and domestic supplies | ✅ |
| Reverse charge on purchases | A bill can be marked "we pay GST" for supplies under reverse charge (transport by a GTA, legal services, services bought from abroad, notified goods): the vendor is owed only the value, while the GST is posted as input credit against a "GST payable under reverse charge" account. Services from abroad are treated as imports (IGST, in rupees at the bill's rate). From an unregistered supplier a self-invoice is numbered (SI/2026-27/0001) and downloadable as a PDF. GSTR-3B shows table 3.1(d), and that GST is payable in cash rather than from credit | ✅ |
| Input tax credit eligibility | Each taxed bill line says whether its GST can be claimed: eligible, blocked under section 17(5) (cars, food, memberships, gifts, building works…) or otherwise ineligible. Items can be marked blocked so their lines always are. GST that can't be claimed is booked as part of the cost (and of what stock cost), never as input credit, and GSTR-3B shows it in 4(B)(1) apart from the credit taken | ✅ |
| 180-day rule for input credit | Rule 37: suppliers must be paid within 180 days of their invoice or the credit taken is reversed. Reports → GST → 180-day rule lists bills in their last 30 days, those to reverse (in proportion to what's unpaid) and those whose credit can be taken back once paid; GSTR-3B reverses it in 4(B)(2) for the month the 180 days end and takes it back in 4(D)(1) when paid, and the dashboard warns | ✅ |
| Imports: bill of entry | Goods imported are entered as a bill from customs (or the clearing agent) with its bill of entry number, date and port code: lines with IGST are the value it's worked out on (owed to the overseas supplier on their own bill, so not owed here), lines without tax are the duties. Customs is owed the duties and the IGST; the IGST is input credit (always IGST, as imports are inter-state) and the duties are cost | ✅ |
| GST registration per party | Each customer and vendor can say how GST treats them: registered, composition dealer, SEZ unit or developer, unregistered business, consumer, overseas, or UN body/embassy (UIN), or leave it to be worked out from the GSTIN and country. It's checked against the GSTIN and address; invoices to an SEZ customer must be SEZ supplies (chosen for you, under LUT if you have one; overseas customers get exports), and bills from a composition dealer can't carry GST | ✅ |
| Registration numbers | Settings keep the kind of business and its PAN, TAN, CIN (or LLPIN) and IEC, each checked for format; the PAN must match the GSTIN and the kind of business (its 4th letter), and the CIN the kind of company. Invoices print the PAN and CIN/LLPIN. Customers and vendors can have a PAN too (checked against their GSTIN), used for TDS when they have no GSTIN | ✅ |
| Bank accounts with IFSC lookup | The organisation's bank account (printed on invoices) and each vendor's (for paying them) are kept with the account name, number and IFSC; the bank and branch fill in from the IFSC through Razorpay's free IFSC directory (the RBI list), kept a month, and can be typed when it's unknown | ✅ |
| People, WhatsApp and delivery addresses | Customers and vendors keep up to 10 people to deal with (designation, email, phone, WhatsApp; one the main contact), a WhatsApp number used for every WhatsApp message when it isn't the phone, up to 20 named delivery addresses (filled from the PIN code) to pick as an invoice's ship-to, and a vendor's usual TDS section, filled in when paying them | ✅ |
| Signature and seal | Settings keep the authorised signatory's name and designation and upload their signature and the business's seal (PNG or JPEG, checked against the bytes, 256 KB). Invoices, credit notes, debit notes and self-invoices print the signature above "Authorised signatory", the seal beside it, and the name under it | ✅ |
| Payment modes | Receipts and payments record how the money moved: cash, cheque, demand draft, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, card. A cheque or DD keeps its 6-digit number, date (refused once over three months old) and bank; transfers their UTR; UPI its 12-digit reference. Cash only goes through cash accounts (and is taken as the mode there). Receipts and payment advice print it: "By Cheque 004512 dated 08/06/2026 on HDFC Bank" | ✅ |
| Cash limits (s.269ST, s.40A(3)) | Recording cash that takes what one person paid in a day (or towards one invoice) to ₹2,00,000 or more, or what was paid to one person in a day over ₹10,000 (₹35,000 to a vendor marked as plying, hiring or leasing goods carriages, s.40A(3A)), stops with the rule and the amount, and goes through only when confirmed as having happened. Reports → TDS lists the days that crossed the limits, for the tax audit (Form 3CD) | ✅ |
| Proforma invoices and sales orders | Next to quotations, proforma invoices (to ask for payment in advance; the PDF says it isn't a tax invoice) and sales orders, each numbered on its own (QT-, PI-, SO-) and listed apart. A quote can be made into a sales order or proforma with one click (its number becomes the reference), and any of them becomes the invoice | ✅ |
| Purchase orders and goods receipts | Purchase orders (PO-0001) priced like bills, issued to the vendor; goods recorded on goods receipt notes (GRN-0001) against each line, never more than was ordered; the bill made from what came and isn't billed yet, at the order's prices, and the order closed when all of it is billed. An order can be cancelled before anything comes, or closed short after. Goods kept in stock come in on the receipt note (with their batch or serial numbers), at the order's price, owed on "Goods received, not yet billed"; recording the bill clears that instead of bringing them in twice, the bill's price difference going to the stock's value, and voiding the bill puts it back. Orders in another currency bring stock in on the bill | ✅ |
| Delivery challans | Goods moved without an invoice (rule 55: job work, on approval, branch transfer, exhibition, quantity known only on delivery) go on a delivery challan, DC-0001, which must say why. Its PDF names the consignor and consignee with GSTINs, the purpose and place of supply, says it isn't a tax invoice, and comes in the three copies the rule asks for (original for consignee, duplicate for transporter, triplicate for consignor). A sales order can become a challan, and a challan the invoice. Each copy downloads from the challan's page. "Goods sent" takes the goods out of stock at average cost (batches and serial numbers as a sale would) into a "Goods sent on delivery challan" asset: off hand, still owned. "Goods came back" returns them exactly as they left; invoicing the challan sells what left on it without taking it twice. Goods sent for job work show the date they're due back (a year, CGST s.143) | ✅ |
| Customer account links | One private link per customer showing every invoice they still owe, with its due date, state and PDF, the total per currency, and one UPI QR code that pays the whole rupee balance at once. Their statement of account for the year so far downloads from the same page. Sent on WhatsApp from the contact page with the message written; shows how often it was opened; can be turned off; never copied into restored backups | ✅ |
| Collections | One list of every customer with something overdue, the most first: how much, how many invoices and how late, when they were last chased and whether they opened their link. One tap sends a WhatsApp message with their account link (all their invoices, one UPI code), or emails a reminder for each overdue invoice; either way the chase is recorded | ✅ |
| Quotes | Estimates for customers, priced with the same lines and GST rules as invoices; sent, accepted or declined, expired past their date, emailed to the customer as a PDF, and turned into a draft invoice in one step | ✅ |
| Recurring bills | Rent, subscriptions and other repeat bills made from a template on a schedule, as drafts or recorded, catching up on missed dates; approvals still apply | ✅ |
| Credit limits | A most-owed amount per customer, in their currency; the customer page shows how much is used, and an invoice that would go over it shows a warning before saving or issuing | ✅ |
| Weekly summary | Owners and Admins can get an email every Monday: cash, what's owed either way, profit this year and what needs attention | ✅ |
| Payment reminders | Email a customer what's still owed on an invoice, when it was due and what's been paid, with an optional note; the invoice shows how many reminders went and when; or automatically for overdue invoices, some days after the due date, then every so many days, up to a limit | ✅ |
| Recurring invoices | Weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly schedules from a template invoice, ending on a date or after a count, raised as drafts or issued, with what's coming next | ✅ #71 |
| Credit notes and refunds | Against the original invoice (some or all of its lines, tax included); the credit comes off the invoice, then goes onto another invoice or is refunded. Each downloads as a PDF for the customer: both GSTINs, the invoice it's against and that invoice's date, HSN/SAC per line, the tax, the amount in words and a signature block; emailed to the customer from the list | ✅ #67 |
| TDS on vendor payments | Tax deducted at source when paying contractors, professionals and landlords: section (194C, 194J, 194I, 194H, 194Q, 194A) with its usual rates suggested, worked out on the bills before GST, kept back from the bank and owed on TDS payable while the bills settle in full; a TDS report by section (Form 26Q) and month with the day it's due (challan 281), each deductee's PAN, and the deposit on the dashboard's due dates | ✅ |
| TDS kept back by customers | Receipts where the customer paid less because they kept TDS back: the invoices settle in full, the bank gets what arrived, and the TDS goes to TDS receivable to claim against Form 26AS; the receipt PDF shows it, and the TDS report has a Kept back by customers side with each customer's PAN | ✅ |
| Payment advice | Every vendor payment downloads or emails as a payment advice: the bills it settles by the vendor's own numbers (with ours), anything paid in advance, the TDS kept back and its section, and what reached their bank with the reference, plus a note that the TDS shows in their Form 26AS | ✅ |
| Receipts and payments on WhatsApp | Any posted receipt or payment opens WhatsApp with the message written: a thank-you naming the invoices for a customer; for a vendor, what reached their bank, the bills by their own numbers, the reference, and any TDS kept back | ✅ |
| MSME 45-day rule | Vendors carry their Udyam number and MSME category; bills from micro and small enterprises get their legal pay-by date (the agreed date, at most 45 days from the bill, or 15 days with no agreement, under MSMED Act section 15). An MSME payments report shows what's overdue, due within the week, paid late, and the taxable value at risk of being disallowed under income-tax section 43B(h) if still unpaid at the year end; the dashboard warns before the deadline | ✅ |
| MSMED interest from late buyers | When the business itself is a micro or small enterprise (Settings: category, Udyam number and the RBI bank rate), invoices paid after the appointed day (agreed date, at most 45 days) show the interest it may claim under section 16: compound with monthly rests at three times the bank rate. It appears on the invoice, on the customer's account link (each invoice and the total, with the Udyam number) and in reminder emails | ✅ |
| Balance confirmations | For the audit: every customer or vendor with a balance on a day (usually the year end) gets a link, by email or WhatsApp, showing that balance as our books have it, with the statement behind it; they agree, or dispute with their own figure and a note, without an account. Answers, who gave them and how often each link was opened are listed under Reports, in English, Hindi or Marathi | ✅ |
| Year-end close | One checklist for closing a financial year (the March close): bank reconciled, no draft entries, balance confirmations asked and answered, MSME bills paid in time (43B(h)), GST details clean, the months closed, and a backup kept, each with where it stands and a link to do it | ✅ |
| Cheque printing | A vendor payment printed onto a standard CTS-2010 cheque leaf: the date in its eight boxes, the payee, the amount in Indian words over two lines and in figures guarded with ** and /-, crossed A/c Payee by default; for what the bank pays after TDS. The print can be nudged in millimetres for the printer, remembered on the device | ✅ |
| Bills entered twice | Recording a bill whose vendor bill number is recorded already for that vendor (however it's typed: SOS/887, sos 887, SOS-0887) is stopped with the bill it duplicates, unless confirmed; a draft also warns of likely duplicates (the same number, or the same total within three days). Saves doubling a cost and its input GST | ✅ |
| Debit notes (purchase returns) | Some of a recorded bill's lines sent back or credited, with the supplier's own credit note number: the expense and its input GST come back off, the credit comes off the bill first, and what's left stays with the vendor to apply to their other bills or have refunded into a bank; voidable until refunded; in payables aging, vendor statements, GSTR-3B and the Tally export (as Debit Note vouchers). ## Quality standards (apply to every feature)

- **Security:** organisation data isolation on every query, role checks on every endpoint, input validation, OWASP Top 10, audit log.
- **Accuracy:** money stored as integers in the smallest unit (no floating-point rounding errors); every posting balanced; posted entries are never edited, only reversed.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard support, screen-reader labels, colour contrast.
- **Performance:** animations use only `transform` and `opacity`; large tables are virtualised; code is split per page.
- **Testing:** unit tests for rules, API tests for every endpoint, end-to-end tests for key flows.
- **Free to run:** GitHub, GitHub Actions and Pages, Neon (PostgreSQL) and Render free tiers.
